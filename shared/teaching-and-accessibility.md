# Teaching, accessibility and safe lab use

## Adoption paths

- **Four courses:** teach each as a 12-week, 3-credit offering. Course 1 is the foundation; courses 2 and 3 can follow in either order; course 4 synthesizes.
- **Three courses:** combine course 2 weeks 1–6 with course 3 weeks 1, 2, 5, 6, 7, 10, and 11. Assign the integrated capstone from course 4 as a graduate seminar.
- **Two courses:** offer course 1 and a selected advanced studio (courses 2–4). Publish a crosswalk to the omitted outcomes and do not promise full technical/security coverage.
- **One survey course:** use weeks 1, 3, 6, 7 of course 1; 2, 5, 8 of course 2; 2, 5, 7 of course 3; 4, 6, 7 of course 4, plus a capstone. Label as an introduction, since breadth does not establish mastery.

## Inclusive delivery

Use semantic Markdown headings, readable tables, text alternatives for diagrams, captions/transcripts for any new video, and high-contrast slide themes. No exercise requires student disclosure of disability, immigration status, personal trauma or protected attributes. A student may present a fictional affected-user position. Offer equivalent spreadsheet or paper analysis for code-based scoring and security-control mapping; allow extra time or asynchronous discussion where required by local accommodations.

## Lab boundaries

The included labs run offline with Python 3 standard library and use only invented data. The prompt-injection lab deliberately demonstrates an **unsafe simulated behavior**, marked in its output, so learners can see why tool permissions must be independent of retrieved text. The lab has no network access and does not send email. Permission to test this repository is not permission to probe a real vendor or university service. Students should never paste real records, credentials or proprietary prompts into public tools.

## Student use of AI assistants

Allow brainstorming and editing if local policy permits, with a short disclosure: tool name, purpose, inputs, generated sections, checks performed and remaining uncertainty. Grade the student's evidence trail and oral explanation. Require source verification against publisher material. Never require use of a commercial tool or a personal account.

## Legal and standards teaching

This is teaching material, not legal advice or a certification course. EU effective dates and national laws change. Instructor checks [official sources](resources.md) at start of term and records the check in [update log](../CHANGELOG.md). ISO full texts are generally copyrighted and often paid; the repository links to publisher overviews without reproducing the protected clauses. Students may use institutional access but are never required to purchase them.

## Assessment authenticity

Answers are public by design. For summative assessment, substitute a new case fact, request a reproducible artifact, and conduct a five-minute oral challenge. Supply rubrics ahead of time. Assess whether students state assumptions, quantify with correct denominators, acknowledge limits and propose an owned action.
