# Week 02: AI management system architecture

**Learning target.** By the end, a learner can explain ai management system architecture, apply it to the CampusAssist case, and defend the evidence in **aims scope, process diagram and evidence schedule**.

**Before class (60–90 min).** Read the first three publisher overviews and the [case dossier](../../../shared/case-campusassist.md); skim additional comparison sources according to the studio task. Write two questions about the evidence you would need to approve the scenario. Required links are free; publisher pages for ISO standards are optional background.

## Reading and standards anchors

- [ISO42001: ISO/IEC 42001:2023, AI management system](https://www.iso.org/standard/81230.html) — Teach original AIMS processes; do not reproduce clauses
- [ISO27001: ISO/IEC 27001:2022, Information security management](https://www.iso.org/standard/27001) — ISMS assurance
- [ISO27701: ISO/IEC 27701:2025, Privacy information management](https://www.iso.org/standard/27701) — PIMS; 2019 edition replaced

**Additional publisher references:** skim the links relevant to this week’s studio; use free overviews where complete standards require purchase.

- [COSO_IC: Internal Control—Integrated Framework](https://www.coso.org/internal-control) — Design control ownership, evidence and monitoring for AI-related objectives.
- [ISO37301: ISO 37301:2021, Compliance Management Systems](https://www.iso.org/standard/75080.html) — Organize obligation inventory, escalation, evidence and management review.
- [NIST_SYS_RMF: SP 800-37 Rev. 2, system Risk Management Framework](https://csrc.nist.gov/pubs/sp/800/37/r2/final) — Distinguish security authorization process from NIST AI RMF voluntary outcomes.

## Teach the concepts

### 1. Concept, decision and evidence

Define scope, policy, objectives, competence, operations, performance evaluation and improvement as a management cycle. Locate this concept in the system workflow: who makes the decision, whose opportunity changes, and what record would verify the account?

### 2. Competing interpretation and limit

Connect AIMS to existing ISMS and privacy practices without claiming they are interchangeable. Ask which plausible competing explanation could produce the same observation. State the sample, role or jurisdiction that limits any conclusion.

### 3. Operational test and owner

Specify control ownership and required records before choosing tooling. Convert the concept into a test or review action with an owner, deadline and observable pass/fail or decision criterion.

### Applied comparison: new governance and security sources

Map AIMS, existing internal control and compliance responsibilities: an obligation owner, service control owner and independent reviewer. Compare NIST system RMF authorization steps with NIST AI RMF outcomes; do not merge the two RMFs.

**Observable evidence:** Integrated process map with scope and evidence boundaries.

## Worked case

The university has an ISMS but no AI inventory or impact review. **Instructor model:** Scope the AIMS to HSU’s advising uses and link to the existing ISMS. An organizational system can be audited; a model is not an ISO 42001 certificate holder. On the board, distinguish **observed fact**, **assumption**, **potential harm**, **control**, and **unanswered question**. Ask the room for one reason a reasonable reviewer might disagree with the model reasoning. Use the source anchors to identify a relevant outcome without claiming a framework automatically approves the system.

## 150-minute lesson plan

1. **0–10 min — diagnostic:** students state the system boundary and a likely affected person.
2. **10–45 min — mini-lesson:** explain the three concepts above and connect each to a case decision.
3. **45–65 min — worked example:** unpack the case fact, identify who can challenge the decision and what evidence is missing.
4. **65–75 min — break.**
5. **75–115 min — studio:** Build a process map and evidence register for a scoped AIMS. Have each team record one rejected alternative and why.
6. **115–135 min — cross-team review:** trade artifacts; reviewers check assumptions, source fit, affected parties and feasibility.
7. **135–150 min — individual exit check:** submit aims scope, process diagram and evidence schedule and a 100-word defense of the most uncertain claim.

## Student studio instructions

Submit **AIMS scope, process diagram and evidence schedule**. Include a purpose and scope statement; a small table of claims, evidence, owners and limitations; at least one affected-person perspective; and a test or review date. Where numbers are used, show the denominator. Where the question is legal, label jurisdiction and effective date. Cite at least two reading links from above. The case is fictional; do not use real student information.

**Source-application check.** Integrated process map with scope and evidence boundaries. Cite the relevant primary publisher pages above, specify source scope and status, and do not claim that a questionnaire answer or technique label proves effectiveness.

## 75-minute supervised extension

In a scheduled studio or supervised online session, spend 20 minutes gathering the linked publisher evidence, 35 minutes revising **aims scope, process diagram and evidence schedule** against a peer's challenge, and 20 minutes writing an individual note that identifies which claim changed, what evidence caused the change, and the next verification step. The instructor records attendance/engagement under local policy. This extension supplies the additional structured contact time in the suggested 12-week, 3-credit format.

## Facilitator cues and assessment

- Probe the misconception: **“ISO/IEC 42001 certification is a property of a model.”** Request a counterexample grounded in the scenario.
- Strong submission: specific facts and assumptions are separated, at least one plausible alternative is tested, a named actor owns the next step, and the conclusion is appropriately limited.
- Developing submission: lists framework names without tying them to a decision or evidence.
- Extension: change one case fact (vendor role, data source, user population or deployment scale); ask which conclusion must change and which remains robust.
