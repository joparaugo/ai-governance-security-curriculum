# Instructor guide — AI Assurance, Data Governance, and Evaluation

## Prepare the term

1. Read the [case dossier](../../shared/case-campusassist.md), [source/version notes](../../shared/framework-crosswalk.md) and [teaching/accessibility guidance](../../shared/teaching-and-accessibility.md).
2. Replace local dates and policies in the syllabus; check the source catalog before the term. Read the current EU amendment when teaching legal topics.
3. Assign groups of 3–5 with rotating roles: system owner, affected-person advocate, technical evaluator/security analyst, and independent challenger. Allow a solo equivalent.
4. Release the weekly module and reading links at least a week ahead; use the slide file as an editable starting deck. Module source IDs link to official publisher pages.
5. Use [assignment rubrics](assignments.md) and public [assessment keys](assessments.md) formatively; change case facts for summative exams.

## 150-minute teaching pattern

Opening diagnostic (10 min); concept instruction (35 min); worked scenario (20 min); break (10 min); studio exercise (40 min); cross-team challenge (20 min); learning check and next step (15 min). Each module spells out the week-specific task. Assess the ability to justify a decision under uncertainty, not framework memorization.

Schedule a separate 75-minute supervised studio each week (live or online) to complete the module extension, discuss feedback and revise the deliverable. Check local credit-hour and seat-time rules when adapting this 12-week format.

## Feedback

Return one strength, one evidence gap, and a concrete next action for each artifact. For group work, ask each student for a 150-word individual contribution/reflection. Check that assigned owners and control tests are observable. Invite respectful challenge; avoid requiring students to disclose personal experience with harmful systems.

## Capstone progression

Week 3: case scope and thesis; Week 6: evidence baseline; Week 9: peer challenge; Week 12: final artifact and five-minute defense. The [capstone rubric](assignments.md) grades evidence traceability, stakeholder impact, feasibility, technical validity and communication.

## Topic sensitivities

Use fictional students, no real institutional records. Legal exercises are issue-spotting, not professional legal advice. For a paywalled ISO standard, teach the publisher overview and free NIST/OWASP equivalents, and label any crosswalk as the instructors' interpretation. See [source status](../../shared/resources.md).

## Added assurance boundary sequence

Week 1 separates a vendor assertion from objective, criteria, test and observed evidence. Week 3 records a model/dataset/software component trace using SPDX. Week 8 compares an AI Verify traditional-model test with separately designed agent behavior tests. In week 10 contrast a hosting SOC 2 report with model-performance and fairness evaluation; E-10 contains a marketing claim, not a report. In week 12 students design a NIST SP 800-53A control test and an independent IIA-style review. Require the [assurance scope note](../../templates/assurance-scope-note.md) in the capstone and the scope/period/exception check in feedback.
