# Week 01: What counts as an AI system?

**Learning target.** By the end, a learner can explain what counts as an ai system?, apply it to the CampusAssist case, and defend the evidence in **one-page system card with actors and intended/excluded uses**.

**Before class (60–90 min).** Read the linked publisher overviews and the [case dossier](../../../shared/case-campusassist.md). Write two questions about the evidence you would need to approve the scenario. Required links are free; publisher pages for ISO standards are optional background.

## Reading and standards anchors

- [ISO22989: ISO/IEC 22989:2022, AI concepts and terminology](https://www.iso.org/standard/74296.html) — Common vocabulary
- [ISO23053: ISO/IEC 23053:2022, ML AI system framework](https://www.iso.org/standard/74438.html) — System component boundaries
- [RMF: AI Risk Management Framework 1.0](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/) — Govern, Map, Measure, Manage; do not treat the Playbook as a mandatory checklist

## Teach the concepts

### 1. Concept, decision and evidence

Distinguish a model from a complete sociotechnical system: data, interfaces, people, rules and appeals all affect outcomes. Locate this concept in the system workflow: who makes the decision, whose opportunity changes, and what record would verify the account?

### 2. Competing interpretation and limit

Describe intended use, foreseeable misuse and excluded uses before judging trustworthiness. Ask which plausible competing explanation could produce the same observation. State the sample, role or jurisdiction that limits any conclusion.

### 3. Operational test and owner

Draw the boundary at the actual decision workflow, including the advisor who can override an alert. Convert the concept into a test or review action with an owner, deadline and observable pass/fail or decision criterion.

## Worked case

CampusAssist flags students for outreach; an advisor, not the score alone, chooses contact. **Instructor model:** The score is a model output; the advisor choice and student appeal are part of the system. A system card that omits the advisor workflow would misstate the deployed decision. On the board, distinguish **observed fact**, **assumption**, **potential harm**, **control**, and **unanswered question**. Ask the room for one reason a reasonable reviewer might disagree with the model reasoning. Use the source anchors to identify a relevant outcome without claiming a framework automatically approves the system.

## 150-minute lesson plan

1. **0–10 min — diagnostic:** students state the system boundary and a likely affected person.
2. **10–45 min — mini-lesson:** explain the three concepts above and connect each to a case decision.
3. **45–65 min — worked example:** unpack the case fact, identify who can challenge the decision and what evidence is missing.
4. **65–75 min — break.**
5. **75–115 min — studio:** Teams draw a boundary diagram with upstream data and downstream actions. Have each team record one rejected alternative and why.
6. **115–135 min — cross-team review:** trade artifacts; reviewers check assumptions, source fit, affected parties and feasibility.
7. **135–150 min — individual exit check:** submit one-page system card with actors and intended/excluded uses and a 100-word defense of the most uncertain claim.

## Student studio instructions

Submit **One-page system card with actors and intended/excluded uses**. Include a purpose and scope statement; a small table of claims, evidence, owners and limitations; at least one affected-person perspective; and a test or review date. Where numbers are used, show the denominator. Where the question is legal, label jurisdiction and effective date. Cite at least two reading links from above. The case is fictional; do not use real student information.

## 75-minute supervised extension

In a scheduled studio or supervised online session, spend 20 minutes gathering the linked publisher evidence, 35 minutes revising **one-page system card with actors and intended/excluded uses** against a peer's challenge, and 20 minutes writing an individual note that identifies which claim changed, what evidence caused the change, and the next verification step. The instructor records attendance/engagement under local policy. This extension supplies the additional structured contact time in the suggested 12-week, 3-credit format.

## Facilitator cues and assessment

- Probe the misconception: **“A model card alone describes the whole deployed system.”** Request a counterexample grounded in the scenario.
- Strong submission: specific facts and assumptions are separated, at least one plausible alternative is tested, a named actor owns the next step, and the conclusion is appropriately limited.
- Developing submission: lists framework names without tying them to a decision or evidence.
- Extension: change one case fact (vendor role, data source, user population or deployment scale); ask which conclusion must change and which remains robust.
