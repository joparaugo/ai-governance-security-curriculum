# Week 09: Human oversight and contestability

**Learning target.** By the end, a learner can explain human oversight and contestability, apply it to the CampusAssist case, and defend the evidence in **oversight protocol and appeal pathway**.

**Before class (60–90 min).** Read the linked publisher overviews and the [case dossier](../../../shared/case-campusassist.md). Write two questions about the evidence you would need to approve the scenario. Required links are free; publisher pages for ISO standards are optional background.

## Reading and standards anchors

- [RMF: AI Risk Management Framework 1.0](https://airc.nist.gov/airmf-resources/airmf/) — Govern, Map, Measure, Manage; do not treat the Playbook as a mandatory checklist
- [EU_ACT: Regulation (EU) 2024/1689, AI Act](https://eur-lex.europa.eu/eli/reg/2024/1689/oj/eng) — Read with 2026/1744 and Commission timeline
- [IEEE7001: IEEE 7001-2021, Transparency of autonomous systems](https://standards.ieee.org/ieee/7001/6929/) — Measurable transparency for stakeholders

## Teach the concepts

### 1. Concept, decision and evidence

Specify what a reviewer sees, when intervention is possible, and how overrides are logged. Locate this concept in the system workflow: who makes the decision, whose opportunity changes, and what record would verify the account?

### 2. Competing interpretation and limit

Test whether a human can actually recognize a wrong recommendation under workload and automation bias. Ask which plausible competing explanation could produce the same observation. State the sample, role or jurisdiction that limits any conclusion.

### 3. Operational test and owner

Design a usable notice, appeal and remediation route for the affected student. Convert the concept into a test or review action with an owner, deadline and observable pass/fail or decision criterion.

## Worked case

An advisor has 12 seconds per alert and no explanation for the score. **Instructor model:** Twelve seconds and unexplained scores make override unlikely. A condition might require longer review time, reason display and a sampled override-quality audit. On the board, distinguish **observed fact**, **assumption**, **potential harm**, **control**, and **unanswered question**. Ask the room for one reason a reasonable reviewer might disagree with the model reasoning. Use the source anchors to identify a relevant outcome without claiming a framework automatically approves the system.

## 150-minute lesson plan

1. **0–10 min — diagnostic:** students state the system boundary and a likely affected person.
2. **10–45 min — mini-lesson:** explain the three concepts above and connect each to a case decision.
3. **45–65 min — worked example:** unpack the case fact, identify who can challenge the decision and what evidence is missing.
4. **65–75 min — break.**
5. **75–115 min — studio:** Role-play appeal and redesign the interface decision point. Have each team record one rejected alternative and why.
6. **115–135 min — cross-team review:** trade artifacts; reviewers check assumptions, source fit, affected parties and feasibility.
7. **135–150 min — individual exit check:** submit oversight protocol and appeal pathway and a 100-word defense of the most uncertain claim.

## Student studio instructions

Submit **Oversight protocol and appeal pathway**. Include a purpose and scope statement; a small table of claims, evidence, owners and limitations; at least one affected-person perspective; and a test or review date. Where numbers are used, show the denominator. Where the question is legal, label jurisdiction and effective date. Cite at least two reading links from above. The case is fictional; do not use real student information.

## 75-minute supervised extension

In a scheduled studio or supervised online session, spend 20 minutes gathering the linked publisher evidence, 35 minutes revising **oversight protocol and appeal pathway** against a peer's challenge, and 20 minutes writing an individual note that identifies which claim changed, what evidence caused the change, and the next verification step. The instructor records attendance/engagement under local policy. This extension supplies the additional structured contact time in the suggested 12-week, 3-credit format.

## Facilitator cues and assessment

- Probe the misconception: **“A human click automatically creates meaningful oversight.”** Request a counterexample grounded in the scenario.
- Strong submission: specific facts and assumptions are separated, at least one plausible alternative is tested, a named actor owns the next step, and the conclusion is appropriately limited.
- Developing submission: lists framework names without tying them to a decision or evidence.
- Extension: change one case fact (vendor role, data source, user population or deployment scale); ask which conclusion must change and which remains robust.
